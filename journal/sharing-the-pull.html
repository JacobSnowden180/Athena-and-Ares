Trail log · Field guide

How we share the pull.

A how-to in the order I would tell it to someone standing next to the scooter. Still no product links. The gear is just what we use.

The rig

I ride a GoTrax XR Pro, a plain electric kick scooter that cost about three hundred dollars. I have seen one European dog kick scooter with bicycle wheels, listed around 2,500 euros. I did not need that. The dogs needed a way to pull, and I needed a way to keep the ride mine when the city got tight.

Each dog wears a tactical harness, not mushing gear. I want precise control. The lead is fixed-length and high quality, wrapped around my fist. I let line out or take it in as we go. I do not use a retractable.

Where they stand

  • Athena, on my left. Labradane, about 5. Cue: “Athena, Nina, Nina, Nina.”
  • Ares, on my right. Great Pyrenees and Anatolian Shepherd, about 4. Cue: “Ares, Ares, Ares.”

She is shy until the moment we move, and then she can pull like she means it. He is rowdier, and he has settled some. If one of them is the engine, the other is allowed to match me instead of being dragged into a job.

Three ways the weight moves

  1. Long trek. I use the throttle so they just match my speed. They stay in the work without towing the whole distance.
  2. Both rowdy. They pull a hundred percent of the weight. I let them.
  3. One rowdy. That dog pulls all the weight. The other matches my pace.

Speed follows the environment. Seven or eight miles an hour is a pace I have used when it fits. It is not a target I announce at the start.

The length of the lead

Short when I want control. Long when they can fan out and breathe. Corners, other dogs, and anything wet on the asphalt are short-lead moments. A clear stretch on a dry night is when the line can go out.

Before anyone rolls

  • About two hours after a big meal. Bloat is the reason.
  • No pavement while rainwater is still on it.
  • Boots if the weather is very hot or very cold.
  • Deterrent spray in the bag, because off-leash and stray dogs sometimes chase, and Athena is reactive toward other dogs.
  • Water for the longer ones. Rest is part of the five-mile days, not a reward at the end.

They stop when I want them to. More verbal commands are the next thing I want to teach. I will write those down when they are reliable, and not before.

The half-mile apartment lap, with the grassy sniff and potty stop, is still the daily floor. The guide is for the nights we go farther than that.
