The dogs

Two very different good dogs.

Athena is the quiet one who becomes the engine. Ares is the goofball who has learned some manners. Both are healthy, on a preventative, and they see the vet regularly.

Athena · the left

A shy dog, until the harness goes on.

Athena is a Labradane, a Labrador and Great Dane mix, about 5. She is shy, subtle, and quiet. Around the house she is a lazy bones. When it is time to go, she is the big mama, and she really pulls.

Other dogs set her off. That is part of why the rides stay precise, and why I carry deterrent spray for off-leash and stray dogs that might give chase. She is not a project to be dramatized. She is a dog who needs the space to work without a surprise in her face.

She picks the Jeep’s front seat on her own. On the scooter she runs on my left. The cue to send her that way is a small chant of her name and a word that belongs only to her: “Athena, Nina, Nina, Nina.”

She eats about 3 cups a day.

Ares · the right

A big, physical goofball.

Ares is a Great Pyrenees and Anatolian Shepherd mix, about 4. He will body-slam you. He is also a good boy who knows his place. Rowdy, yes, and he has calmed down some. The calm is not the same thing as being still.

He prefers the Jeep’s back seat. On the scooter he runs on my right. The cue is just his name, said like a short drum: “Ares, Ares, Ares.”

He eats about 4 cups a day. On the hot nights the boots go on his feet the same as hers, and the water comes with them.

Together

Left and right, every day since it started.

It took a long time after I got the scooter. We have gone every day since. Five miles around the neighborhood is an easy outing, with rest and water. The daily apartment lap is shorter: about half a mile, with a sniff and a potty stop at a grassy field.
