A field guide

Urban mushing, as we actually do it.

I want to teach this someday. These are the notes: the humble scooter, the tactical harness, the fixed lead, and the way throttle and dog power take turns. Nothing here is for sale.

What it is

Urban mushing, or scooterjoring, is the name I use. The dogs are in front, in harness, on a lead. I am on a kick scooter. Sometimes the motor carries the speed. Sometimes the dogs do. The street decides more than any of us does.

I have had us around seven or eight miles an hour when the way is clear. Five miles around the neighborhood is an easy outing, with rest and water along it. None of that is a record. It is the shape of an ordinary good day.

The scooter

The scooter is a GoTrax XR Pro, an ordinary electric kick scooter, about three hundred dollars. I think the setup is unusual because it is not a purpose-built dog scooter. The only other approach I have seen is a European dog kick scooter with bicycle wheels, somewhere around 2,500 euros. I stayed with the humble one.

There is no link to buy one here, and no preference beyond what I ride. The point is that the practice did not require a specialty machine.

Harness and lead

I use tactical harnesses, not mushing gear. This is real urban mushing, in a city, and I want precise control rather than handing the ride over to the dogs. The lead is a high-quality fixed-length line, wrapped around my fist. I adjust the length as we go. There are no retractable leashes.

Scooter

GoTrax XR Pro. Electric kick scooter. About $300. The deck I already had, not a dog-sport chassis.

Harness

Tactical, one each. Chosen for steering a city ride, not for a trail sled.

Lead

Fixed length, in the fist. Shortened for control, lengthened when they can spread out and breathe.

Boots

For very hot weather and very cold weather. The rest of the time, bare paws on dry pavement.

How the work is shared

I mix throttle and dog power, and I set the speed by the environment. There are three usual ways the weight moves.

  1. On long treks I use the throttle so the dogs just match my speed. They are out, they are working, and they are not being asked to tow the whole night.
  2. When they are rowdy, I let them pull a hundred percent of the weight. The motor can rest. The dogs came to run.
  3. When only one of them is rowdy, that dog pulls all the weight, and the other matches my pace.

Athena is often the one who becomes the engine once she decides the lazy part of the day is over. Ares brings the body. Either of them can be the one pulling. The other is allowed to simply travel.

Lead length

The line stays in my hand the whole time. Wrapped around the fist, it can be taken in or given out without a mechanism. Shorter means control: a corner, a surprise, a dog I do not know. Longer means they can fan out beside each other and breathe, which matters on a warm night.

I do not use a retractable. A retractable decides the length by itself, and this ride is not supposed to be decided by a spring.

The words

They already stop when I want them to stop. The directional cues are specific.

  • Athena runs on the left. Her cue is “Athena, Nina, Nina, Nina.”
  • Ares runs on the right. His cue is “Ares, Ares, Ares.”

Teaching more verbal commands is a next goal. The notes will grow when those words are real, not before.

What a day looks like

There is a daily apartment lap of about half a mile, with a sniff-and-potty stop at a grassy field. That one is the promise. The longer neighborhood runs, up to an easy five miles with rest and water, are the days when everyone has more to give.

Some nights stay short. A couple of miles is a real night too. On a hot one, four miles has meant four cold bottles of water, and the boots were already on.

Safety

These are the rules I do not treat as optional. They are here so the guide is honest, not so the page can sound severe.

  1. 01

    Wait about two hours after a big meal before running. The reason is bloat.

  2. 02

    If rain is still sitting on the asphalt, skip the pavement until it is gone.

  3. 03

    Use dog boots in very hot weather and in very cold weather.

  4. 04

    Carry deterrent spray. Off-leash and stray dogs sometimes give chase, and Athena is reactive toward other dogs.

  5. 05

    Rest and water belong in the longer rides. Five miles is easy only if those stops are real.

  6. 06

    They stop when I want. If that ever stops being true, the ride is over for the day.

Both dogs are healthy. They are on a preventative, and they see the vet regularly. Athena eats about 3 cups a day. Ares eats about 4.

How it started

It started when I got an electric scooter. Teaching the dogs took a long time. We have done it every day since. The daily lap is how the long teaching stays kept.

If you want the same shape of day with your own dogs, start from the safety list and from the fact that this took time. The gear can be ordinary. The habit is the part that is not.
